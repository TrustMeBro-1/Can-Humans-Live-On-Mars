How Could Humans Live on Mars?

Going to Mars takes around 2–3 years depending on the mission. When going to space the astronauts face around 250–700 times more radiation than we experience on Earth, because when astronauts go to space Earth's magnetic sphere is not protecting them.

For them to live on Mars they can live in a Mars Space Station and have remote controlled robots help manage it. Another way to make Mars habitable is by extracting the oxygen out of Mars's majority air component, Carbon Dioxide, by using a rover that does just that called MOXIE.

To build houses on Mars to house astronauts they'll use StarCrete — made of Starch and Mars soil — and it'll be twice as times stronger than concrete. Also you don't stack them like bricks, you use 3D printing technology to build the housing units!

nasa.gov — 6 Technologies NASA is Advancing
🚀

Technologies to Send Humans to Mars

Most likely we'll send humans to Mars by the early 2030s and it'll be around a 2 year trip. Since Mars is 140 million miles away from Earth, humans would need to build highly advanced propulsion systems based on nuclear energy — either a nuclear electric rocket or a nuclear thermal propulsion.

Second, we'd need to build a massive inflatable heat shield to protect astronauts from heat entering Mars's atmosphere. Third, a Martian space suit that'll protect us from extreme temperatures and radiation, and allow for lots of mobility.

Fourth, an advanced rover that's like an RV but for astronauts to do testing without being harmed by Mars's climate. Fifth, we'll need a reliable power source like nuclear energy. Lastly, we'll need laser communication to transfer data in high definition and quickly.

harvardmagazine.com — Harvard Mars Habitability Research
🎓

Harvard Mars Habitability & Space Medicine

Astronauts would need highly advanced medical technology that can detect health problems early and treat them efficiently and effectively. Since getting help from Earth takes 20+ minutes per signal, astronauts must be able to handle medical emergencies entirely on their own.

The technology we obtain after going to Mars can also be used back on Earth to treat trauma patients and multi-organ failure — meaning the mission benefits all of humanity, not just the astronauts on Mars.

They'll also build technology to protect astronauts from harmful radiation rays and extreme temperature changes, since Mars has no magnetic field to shield against solar and cosmic radiation like Earth does.

03 — TECHNOLOGY NEEDED

What It Would Take

The technology that would be needed to make living on Mars possible.

01

Nuclear Propulsion Systems

A nuclear electric rocket or nuclear thermal propulsion to get humans to Mars — 140 million miles away — in a reasonable time. Current chemical rockets would take too long and expose astronauts to too much radiation during the journey.

02

MOXIE — Oxygen from CO₂

A rover-based device that extracts oxygen from Mars's majority air component, Carbon Dioxide. NASA has already tested this with a small MOXIE unit on the Perseverance rover. A scaled-up version could supply entire colonies.

03

StarCrete 3D-Printed Habitats

Houses on Mars built from StarCrete — made of Starch and Mars soil — which is twice as strong as concrete. These are 3D printed rather than stacked like bricks, making construction possible without a huge human workforce on the surface.

04

Inflatable Heat Shield

A massive inflatable heat shield to protect astronauts and their craft from the extreme heat generated when entering Mars's atmosphere during landing.

05

Mars Space Station & Advanced Spacesuits

A pressurized Mars Space Station with remote controlled robots to manage operations. Combined with next-gen Martian spacesuits that protect from extreme temperatures, radiation, and dust — while still allowing full mobility for long working hours on the surface.

06

Nuclear Power & Laser Communication

A reliable nuclear power source to keep the base running day and night through Mars's dust storms. Paired with laser communication systems to transfer data to and from Earth in high definition and at high speeds.

Summary & Future Research

📋 Summary of Findings

In my research I learned that humans living on Mars is actually being worked on right now. The trip takes 2 to 3 years and exposes astronauts to up to 700 times more radiation than Earth. To get there we need nuclear propulsion systems to travel the 140 million miles and an inflatable heat shield to survive entering Mars's atmosphere.

Once there, houses will be 3D printed using StarCrete made from Martian soil, oxygen will be made by a device called MOXIE that takes Mars's CO₂ atmosphere and extracts the oxygen. Also, astronauts will need advanced medical technology that can detect and treat health problems without any help from Earth since signals take around 20 minutes to reach us.
